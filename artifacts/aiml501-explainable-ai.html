Opening the black box: how we explain and check large language models

Chatbots like GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama make decisions no one can fully read. This map shows why that is hard, what the four biggest labs are doing about it, and how testing turns an explanation into something you can trust.

What explainable AI means, and why it matters

Explainable AI is the work of showing people why a model gave an answer, in a way they can understand and check.

TrustDoctors, judges, and customers need reasons before they act on an answer.
SafetyEngineers can only fix a failure they can see.
FairnessExplanations reveal when a model leans on race, gender, or dialect.
The lawThe EU AI Act gives people affected by high-risk AI decisions a right to a clear explanation.

Four promises a good explanation should keep

ExplanationIt gives reasons or evidence for the output.
MeaningfulIts intended reader can understand it.
AccurateIt matches how the model really decided.
Knows its limitsIt flags when the model is out of its depth.

Based on NIST's four principles of explainable AI (Phillips et al., 2021). Most of today's problems break the third promise.

How validation and metrics turn an explanation into trust

An explanation is only a claim until it is tested. These four checks, run in order and repeated, are how labs and users decide whether a model, and its explanation, can be relied on.

A failed check sends the team back to the data, the model, or the explanation, so the loop never really ends.

What this means for anyone using AI

A model's own explanation is a clue, not proof.

When tested, reasoning models admitted using a planted hint only 25% to 39% of the time.

Looking inside is real but early.

Labs can now find millions of concepts inside models, yet the best tools give clear answers for only about a quarter of the prompts tried.

Trust comes from testing, not from words.

Ask for the metrics: recall and precision on your own data, bias tests, red-team results, and how the explanation itself was checked.
